02 The Challenge

Feature-rich.
Experience-poor.

As Club grew rapidly, new capabilities were continuously layered on top of the existing structure. The result: a powerful platform that was increasingly hard to navigate. Users were getting lost, missions were being missed, and rewards felt unclear.

Low Mission Visibility

Available missions were buried under navigation layers. New members couldn't quickly discover what was available, leading to high drop-off in the first session.

Reward Ambiguity

Three distinct reward types — cash, gift cards, and free products — were presented inconsistently. Users struggled to understand what they'd earn and when they'd receive it.

No Sense of Progress

Without a visible progression system, members had no reason to return after completing a mission. There was no momentum — no path to better rewards or higher status.

Brand Inconsistency

Each brand page felt like a different product. Without a template system, experience quality varied wildly — making it hard for users to build trust across brand communities.

03 Key Insight
People don't engage with features — they engage with progress. The redesign needed to make earning feel inevitable.
Derived from usage data, session recordings, and qualitative interviews with community members

When we analysed why users dropped off, it wasn't confusion about what the app does — it was a lack of forward momentum. Users understood missions. They understood rewards. What they couldn't see was where they were going. The redesign centred on making progress tangible, rewards visible, and the next step always obvious.

04 Objectives

Four goals.
One coherent experience.

Rather than patching individual screens, clear design objectives were defined to guide every decision — from information architecture to micro-interaction.

01

Reduce time-to-first-mission

New users should find and start their first mission within the first session. Eliminate the onboarding friction causing early drop-off.

02

Make reward types instantly clear

Cash, gift cards, and free products should each have a distinct visual identity — no ambiguity about what a user is working towards.

03

Create a visible progression system

Design a Clubs & Levels mechanic that gives users a sense of status, momentum, and a reason to keep participating after their first mission.

04

Standardise the brand experience

Build a template system that ensures every brand page delivers a consistent, high-quality experience while allowing meaningful brand customisation.

05 Experience Principles

The principles that
shaped every decision.

Before touching any screen, six experience principles were defined as a shared design language for the team.

🎯

Clarity Over Capability

Show users what they can do right now — not everything they could ever do. Surface the most relevant missions and hide the rest until needed.

💰

Reward Visibility

Every mission should show its reward upfront, above the fold. Users should never have to hunt for what they're earning — it should be impossible to miss.

⚡

Momentum by Design

Every completed action should lead naturally to the next. Use progress indicators, streaks, and level-up moments to keep users moving forward.

🤝

Trust Through Transparency

Be explicit about how rewards are earned, when they'll be paid, and what's required. Ambiguity kills participation — clarity builds it.

🏆

Status as Motivation

People care about where they stand. Leaderboards, club levels, and ambassador tiers should feel aspirational — within reach, worth chasing.

🧩

System Consistency

Whether in their first brand community or their tenth, the experience language should feel familiar. Patterns should be learnable and predictable.

08 Screens & Flows

The work in detail.

Four core areas were redesigned. Each group shows the screens alongside the problem they solve and the reasoning behind the design decisions.

A Overview & Brand Discovery

The brand page was the most visited screen but the least optimised. It needed to act as a command centre — showing the most relevant missions, community activity, and earnings in a single view.

Brand Page Template

Standardised layout with Missions, Wall, Earn, and Activity tabs — consistent across all 550+ brands, with brand-specific customisation in the cover and bio.

Full App Template

The unified template approach reduced brand setup time significantly while ensuring every community member gets a predictable, trustworthy experience.

B Missions

Missions are the heart of the platform. The challenge was to make available missions instantly scannable, showing reward, effort, and deadline at a glance — without overwhelming users with detail.

Mission Overview

A clean feed showing Available, In Progress, and Needs Attention states — reward amount always visible in the top right.

Mission Header

Reward amount, type, and deadline shown above the fold on every mission. Users should never scroll to find what they're working towards.

Mission List

Compact card format allows users to scan multiple missions quickly. The reward badge is colour-coded by type for immediate recognition.

C Rewards System

Three reward types — Cash, Gift Cards, and Free Products — each needed a distinct visual identity. The redesign introduced a reward icon system and consistent card layouts that make the reward type unmistakable.

Cash Reward

Green currency icon with real-time earning estimate shown inline.

Gift Card

Distinct card icon with brand logo — shows fixed value clearly upfront.

Free Product

Product imagery with estimated retail value and shipping indicator.

Icon System

Three icons distinct in shape, colour, and metaphor — learnable in one session, recognisable forever.

D Templates & System Components

To scale consistency across 550+ brands, a mission template system was built — allowing brands to create high-quality missions from proven structures rather than starting from scratch.

Template Band

A horizontal scrollable band of mission templates categorised by goal type — Content, Sales, Community, and Feedback — dramatically reducing brand setup time.

Value & Stats Component

A reusable data visualisation component showing mission reach, engagement, and ROI — giving brands confidence and users context.

09 Decision Highlights

Why we chose
what we chose.

Product design is a series of trade-offs. Here are the most critical decisions made during this project — and the thinking behind each one.

01

Reward amount above the fold — always

Early designs showed reward information after the mission description. Testing revealed that users judged whether a mission was worth their time in under 3 seconds — and the most important signal was the reward. Moving reward to the top increased mission start rates significantly. This ran counter to conventional "lead with value proposition" thinking, but the data was clear: in a marketplace, the reward IS the value proposition.

Interaction Design
02

Three reward icons — not a generic tag

The original design used a single "reward" icon with text indicating type. This required users to read — and reading takes cognitive load. The new system uses three distinct icons with different shapes, colours, and metaphors (coin for cash, card for gift, box for product). In usability testing, users identified reward type correctly 94% of the time without reading any text. A proper icon system over a shortcut paid off immediately in comprehension speed.

Visual Design
03

Template system before AI suggestions

There was appetite to build an AI-powered mission suggestion system. While compelling, it would have required significant backend work and still couldn't solve the core problem: brands didn't know what mission structure worked. The template system gave brands access to proven, highest-performing mission formats immediately. Faster to ship, faster to validate, more reliable than a model trained on limited early data. We chose a solved problem over an exciting one.

Product Strategy
04

Clubs & Levels: gamification with meaning

Loyalty points and levels are ubiquitous — and often hollow. The Clubs & Levels design avoided vanity mechanics by tying every level to a real, tangible perk (higher commission rates, earlier access to product seeding). Users needed to feel that levelling up meant something outside the app. Working with the product team, each level milestone was mapped to a concrete brand-side benefit, making progression genuinely motivating rather than cosmetically satisfying.

11 Challenges

What made this hard.

Every project has constraints that shape decisions. Understanding these helps explain why certain trade-offs were made.

Legacy content at scale

550+ brands had existing mission libraries, brand pages, and community content. Any structural change had to migrate gracefully — breaking changes were not an option when real users depended on continuity.

Feature parity across platforms

The app runs on iOS and Android via Flutter. Platform-specific UI conventions had to be balanced against the need for a consistent cross-platform design system — especially for gestures and navigation patterns.

Two-sided stakeholder balance

Club serves both brands (who want customisation and control) and community members (who want simplicity and clarity). Design decisions that help one side often create tension for the other — requiring constant calibration.

12 Reflection
What I took away from this
Designing for a two-sided marketplace taught me that the best product decisions feel invisible to both sides. When the community member's experience is joyful, brands benefit automatically — and vice versa.
The most impactful change in this project wasn't a single screen — it was the decision to restructure the information architecture around the user's emotional journey rather than the product's feature set. That shift in framing unlocked every other improvement that followed.

The other major learning: constraint is a design tool. The template system existed because we couldn't afford to redesign 550 brand pages individually. That constraint forced a scalable, principled solution that turned out to be better than the bespoke approach would have been.
