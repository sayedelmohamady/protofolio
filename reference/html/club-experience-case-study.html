Building a brand ambassador

The Product

Brands needed a direct line
to the people who love them.

Ambassador marketing existed — but it was fragmented, manual, and hard to scale. Brands managed influencers over DMs and spreadsheets. There was no dedicated product that let a brand launch missions, track participation, and reward real-world engagement at scale. Club was built to solve that.

04 Experience Principles

The principles that
shaped every decision.

Before touching any screen, six experience principles were defined as a shared design language for the team.

Clarity Over Capability

Surface what's relevant now. Hide the rest until needed.

Reward Visibility

Show the reward upfront — users should never have to hunt for what they're earning.

Momentum by Design

Each action leads to the next. Progress indicators and streaks keep momentum alive.

Trust Through Transparency

Be explicit about how rewards are earned and when they're paid. Ambiguity kills participation.

Status as Motivation

Leaderboards and tiers should feel aspirational — within reach, worth chasing.

System Consistency

The experience language should feel familiar across every brand — learnable and predictable.

09 Decision Highlights

The thinking behind
the key calls.

Building from zero means every pattern is a choice. Here are the decisions that shaped the product most — and why we made them.

01

Reward amount above the fold — always

Users judge a mission in under 3 seconds. The reward is the value proposition — so it leads.

Interaction Design
02

Three reward icons — not a generic tag

Icon + text label required reading. Three distinct icons — coin, card, box — got 94% recognition without any text.

Visual Design
03

Template system before AI suggestions

AI sounded exciting but couldn't solve the real problem. Templates gave brands proven mission formats immediately — faster to ship, faster to validate.

Product Strategy
04

Clubs & Levels: gamification with meaning

Every level tied to a real perk — higher commission, earlier product access. Progress had to mean something outside the app.

UX Strategy
10 Reflection
What I took away from this
Building a two-sided marketplace from scratch taught me that the best product decisions feel invisible to both sides. When the ambassador's experience is clear and rewarding, brands win automatically — and vice versa.
The most important decision in this project wasn't a screen — it was choosing to structure the entire information architecture around the user's emotional journey rather than the product's feature list. That framing shaped everything else.

The other big learning: constraints produce better systems. The mission template system was born out of the need to scale to 550+ brands without building bespoke experiences for each. That constraint forced a principled, reusable solution that turned out to be more powerful than a custom approach would have been.
