01 Overview

A progression system for
ambassador engagement.

Club Levels adds tiers, points, and unlocks to the Club ambassador experience so members can see where they stand and what comes next. Status and perks scale with real participation—linking quick wins to sustained advocacy—on iOS and Android.

03 The Problem

Engagement had no engine. Users acted once — then disappeared.

No progress. No status. No reason to stay.

Inconsistent engagement

Spikes at launch, then drops. Nothing to sustain participation.

No sense of progress

No visibility into standing, distance covered, or what to work toward.

Post-mission drop-off

First mission done — then gone. No pull to come back.

No segmentation for brands

Everyone looked the same. No way to identify or reward top performers.

04 Goal

Give participation
a direction.

Design a system that makes long-term engagement feel worthwhile — for ambassadors and the brands who need to identify their best ones.

Encourage continuous engagement

A reason to return — not just when missions drop, but as a habit.

Create a visible sense of progress

Make it clear where each ambassador stands, how far they've come, and what they're working toward next.

Help brands identify top users

A clear signal of who's most engaged — so brands can treat them differently.

Keep the system simple

Earn points, level up, unlock more. No confusion.

05 Solution

Introducing Levels.

08 UX Decisions

The thinking behind
the key calls.

Every design choice in Levels was deliberate. These are the three principles that shaped the most critical decisions.

A

Clarity of Progress

The biggest risk in a progression system is ambiguity. If users can't tell at a glance where they stand, the motivation collapses. Every screen in Levels shows exactly where the user is, how many points they need to advance, and what they'll unlock when they do. No navigation required. No mental math. The progress bar is always visible, always accurate, always encouraging.

Information Design
B

Avoiding Gamification Noise

Levels could have been a badge farm — daily streaks, achievement walls, arbitrary challenges. We deliberately avoided that. Every level and every perk had to map to something real: a higher commission rate, earlier access to products, direct brand collaboration. The progression system earns trust because it delivers value outside the app. Gamification without substance breeds short-term spikes and long-term skepticism.

Product Strategy
C

Motivation Design

The level-up moment is the highest emotional point in the system. It needed to feel earned and celebratory — not mechanical. We designed it as a dedicated full-screen state, not a toast or a notification. The new perks are revealed progressively. The user gets a moment to absorb what they've achieved before moving on. That pause matters — it makes the progression memorable and worth chasing again.

Interaction Design

10 Learnings

What this project
taught me.

  • 01 Progression needs an anchor in reality. The moment we tied levels to real-world perks — not just badges — the system became credible. Abstract gamification fails. Concrete value compounds.
  • 02 Emotional moments need dedicated space. The level-up screen being full-screen wasn't an indulgence — it was the design. Users remember moments, not flows. The celebration earns the next engagement.
  • 03 Simplicity is a feature, not a constraint. The pressure to add more tiers, more badges, more complexity was constant. Resisting that — keeping it to four levels with clear, meaningful thresholds — made the system legible and the progress feel achievable.
  • 04 Progress visibility is a retention mechanism. Showing exactly how close a user is to the next level — the "64% to Gold" bar — was the single most effective element for driving return visits. The gap creates pull.
  • 05 Design for both sides. Levels wasn't just a feature for ambassadors. It gave brand managers a new signal: who are my top 10%? Designing for both audiences at once — without making either feel like an afterthought — was the most interesting challenge in the project.
Closing thought
The best progression systems are invisible when they're working — users just find themselves coming back, moving forward, and feeling like the platform values what they do.
Levels taught me that engagement features only work when they're grounded in real value. A badge without a perk is noise. A level without a benefit is a dead end. When every step in the system means something outside the app, the inside takes care of itself.
