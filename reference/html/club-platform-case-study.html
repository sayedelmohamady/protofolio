02 The Problem

Brands were building blind. Separated from the thing they were building.

Traditional admin dashboards disconnected brands from their actual product. Settings felt abstract. Configuration felt technical. The real brand experience — the thing members would see — disappeared entirely during the setup process.

The result: longer time-to-launch, more support tickets, and brands who didn't feel ownership over what they'd built.

Invisible product

Brands configured settings without ever seeing what their club would look like to members.

Abstract workflows

Reward rules and mission logic felt technical — not tied to the lived experience of ambassadors.

Slow onboarding

New brands took too long to get their first mission live. Friction compounded at every step.

No sense of ownership

Brands didn't feel like their club was truly theirs. The gap between setup and reality was too wide.

03 Core Insight
“
Brands should build the experience
while seeing the experience.
The central design principle that reshaped every workflow on the platform

Live Preview at Center

The brand's actual Club page — exactly as members see it — sits at the center of every workflow. Not a mockup. Not a preview toggle. The real thing.

Modal Overlay Architecture

Every major action — e-commerce connections, mission creation, reward setup — surfaces as a modal over the live page. You never leave the context of your club.

Purposeful Configuration

Every setting has visual consequence. Every input maps to a visible change. Configuration stops feeling technical and starts feeling like design.

04 Solution

Club operates as a dual-sided product: brands manage their community and run campaigns from the web platform, while ambassadors discover, complete missions, and earn rewards through the mobile app. The design challenge was making both sides feel effortless — and deeply connected.

08 UX Decisions

The thinking behind
the key calls.

Five principles that shaped the most consequential design decisions on the platform.

A

Build While Experiencing

The brand's live club page never leaves the screen. Modal overlays preserve context while enabling configuration — you always know exactly what you're building toward.

Core Architecture
B

Scalable Wizard Patterns

Onboarding and setup flows use adaptive wizards — fewer steps for known users, full guidance for new ones. Complexity is hidden until it's needed.

Progressive Disclosure
C

Geographic Segmentation

Brands need to reward local ambassadors differently than global followers. A geographic filter system makes regional targeting first-class — not a workaround.

Market Strategy
D

Approval as Trust Signal

Submission review is designed to feel fair, not bureaucratic. Clear criteria, fast feedback, and visible status keep ambassadors motivated through the wait.

Motivation Design

09 Impact

What changed
when we shipped it.

The shift from disconnected admin dashboard to contextual brand builder had measurable effects across onboarding, comprehension, and long-term platform engagement.

Faster onboarding, stronger comprehension

Brands reached their first live mission significantly faster after the modal architecture replaced the legacy dashboard flow. Support tickets dropped as setup became self-evident.

Clearer brand ownership

Brands felt the club was truly theirs — because they built it while seeing it.

Scalable approval workflows

Internal review flows scaled with ambassador growth — no operational breakpoints.

Eliminated the build-experience gap

The core problem — brands lost in abstraction — was architecturally solved, not patched.

10 Learnings

What this project
taught me.

  • 01 Context collapse kills comprehension.

    The moment a brand leaves the context of their product to configure it, comprehension breaks. The architecture has to preserve context — not fight it.

  • 02 Dual-sided products need a single design principle.

    "Build while experiencing" worked for brands. "Always a clear next step" worked for ambassadors. One principle per side, not one shared muddle.

  • 03 The loop is the product.

    Community-led growth isn't a feature — it's the business model. Every design decision has to serve the loop or it's noise.

  • 04 Consistency compounds.

    Five core patterns covering 95% of interactions means brands learn once. That's not a shortcut — it's a competitive advantage baked into the UX.

  • 05 Head of Design is also product strategy.

    At this scale, design decisions are business decisions. The geographic segmentation system was a market strategy call — it just happened to live in the UI.

Closing thought
When a platform disappears — brands just build, ambassadors just act — the design has done its job.
The best interface is the one you stop noticing because the outcome is too good to look away from.
